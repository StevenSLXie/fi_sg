# Proposed Outline

Working structure as of 2026-05-11. Provisional, intended for discussion. Each chapter lists its job, core claim, primary story / number anchor, and source essays.

Total: 19 chapters across 5 parts.

## Part I: The Arithmetic of Freedom

The job of Part I: make the math feel concrete and survivable. Every chapter must land at least one story anchor and one number anchor. Reader should leave Part I believing FI is arithmetic, not magic.

### 1. When Work Becomes Optional

- **Job**: Separate FI from RE. FI is optionality, not an age claim.
- **Core claim**: The interesting question is when work becomes optional, not when work ends.
- **Story anchor**: Author writing this book at 53% of the way to FI — mid-journey, not victory lap.
- **Number anchor**: Contrast typical RE statistics (rare) with partial-FI states (more common than people think).
- **Singapore specifics**: CPF retirement age vs author-defined FI threshold; CPF LIFE payout age vs employment cessation age.
- **Source essays**: 014 (FI is not only for the few).

### 2. Two Graduates, One Spreadsheet

- **Job**: Make savings rate the unit of progress, not salary.
- **Core claim**: A higher earner with a low savings rate can reach FI later than a lower earner with a high savings rate.
- **Story anchor**: Two Singaporean graduates, one high-income investment-bank analyst with high fixed costs, one ordinary graduate with a high savings rate; author's own 55% -> 72% trajectory.
- **Number anchor**: Savings-rate-to-years-of-FI table; 30% vs 50% savings-rate comparison; 72% means one year of work funds about 2.5 years of current life.
- **Singapore specifics**: Including CPF and mortgage principal in savings rate; public primary school as an expense cliff; CPF as wealth but not cash.
- **Bookkeeping section**: Minimal bridge only. Full method moved to Chapter 3.
- **Source essays**: 007 (big-tech FI thought experiment), 011 (4% savings rate bookkeeping), 013 (two years bookkeeping lessons), 014 (FI is not only for the few).

### 3. What Gets Measured Gets Retired (NEW)

- **Job**: Turn the introduction's monthly bookkeeping ritual into a practical measurement system.
- **Core claim**: Savings rate is only useful if it is measured honestly. A household needs a simple, sustainable monthly process, not a beautiful spreadsheet that dies after three weeks.
- **Story anchor**: Author's month-end bookkeeping ritual since July 2022; line-by-line bank and credit-card review; one hour per month.
- **Number anchor**: Rolling 12-month savings rate; net worth; total assets, total liabilities, leverage; passive income / spending or investable-assets / spending FI progress.
- **Singapore specifics**: Count CPF as wealth with liquidity caveats; separate mortgage principal from interest; include annual insurance / tax / school-fee lumps through rolling averages.
- **Source essays**: 011 (4% savings rate bookkeeping), 013 (two years bookkeeping lessons).

### 4. Three Point Five, Not Four (NEW)

- **Job**: Take the 4% rule seriously, then qualify it. Give Part I a hard-data spine.
- **Core claim**: 4% is a useful planning anchor for a 30-year horizon and a U.S. 60/40 portfolio. For Singaporeans planning a 40–50-year FI, with different asset mix, currency, and tax treatment, 3.5% is the safer working number.
- **Story anchor**: A retiree who started in a bad market sequence (e.g., 1966 / early-2000s starts) — what actually happened year by year. Sequence-of-returns risk shown, not described.
- **Number anchor**: Trinity-style backtest with explicit scenarios; failure rates at 4% vs 3.5% across horizons; sensitivity to asset allocation and inflation regime.
- **Singapore specifics**: Why 4% imported from U.S. data is fragile when applied to SGD-spending Singaporean portfolios; effect of CPF as a bond-like asset on withdrawal math.
- **Source essays**: 015 (4% rule backtest) — this chapter exists primarily to land that essay.

## Part II: The Singapore FI Stack

The job of Part II: explain Singapore's institutions as financial instruments, not as background. By the end of this part the reader should see CPF, tax, SGD, and HDB as system components a citizen can use deliberately.

### 5. CPF Is Not One Thing

- **Job**: Demystify CPF without writing an encyclopedia, immediately after Chapter 4 brings CPF into the FI asset base.
- **Core claim**: CPF is several financial products sharing a name (OA, SA, MA, RA, CPF LIFE). Count it as wealth, not cash; use the parts deliberately.
- **Story anchor**: Start from the common objection that CPF feels like a tax, then walk through what happens to one paycheque from contribution through age 55 account sorting to CPF LIFE payouts.
- **Number anchor**: Contribution rates by age; OW ceiling 2026; interest rates by account; BRS / FRS / ERS thresholds; BHS; expected CPF LIFE payouts under each plan.
- **Decision questions**: What does each account do? What happens at 55? Should one top up SA or MA? How does CPF fit into bond allocation? When does 1M65-style OA-to-SA transfer help, and when does it damage liquidity?
- **Source essays**: 003 (CPF MA vs SA top-up), 006 (CPF primer).

### 6. Low Taxes Are a Feature

- **Job**: Establish retention rate as the first lever Singapore offers.
- **Core claim**: Low personal income tax + no capital gains tax + no local dividend tax + no estate duty is an enormous FI accelerator that locals routinely under-price.
- **Story anchor**: Author's post-children household tax position (wife near zero, husband partially offset). Compare with hypothetical China or California equivalent for contrast.
- **Number anchor**: Singapore PIT brackets vs China (top 45%) vs California (federal 37% + state 13.3%); after-tax income on a S$200k household across the three jurisdictions.
- **Singapore specifics**: Personal reliefs (qualifying child relief, working mother's child relief, parent relief); SRS tax shelter; CPF top-up reliefs as a tax tool.
- **Needs data**: IRAS current brackets and reliefs.

### 7. The Strong Dollar Under Your Feet (NEW)

- **Job**: Treat SGD as part of the FI stack and part of the FI risk.
- **Core claim**: SGD's structural strength is policy + supply-side institutional drains + demand-side capital inflows. It is not a law of nature; it is a bet on Singapore's continued institutional quality.
- **Story anchor**: SGD/MYR over 20 years and how it powers the JB arbitrage; SGD vs USD over the working life of a current 40-year-old.
- **Number anchor**: MAS official foreign reserves; SGD trade-weighted index; CPF and fiscal-surplus contribution to SGD supply drainage.
- **Singapore specifics**: MAS exchange-rate-band policy explained without jargon; reserves accumulation; why SGD strength enables both regional arbitrage and currency risk on foreign-equity holdings.
- **Source essays**: 010 (why SGD appreciates).

### 8. HDB Is the Shortcut

- **Job**: Reclaim HDB as a financial asset for the FI-conscious citizen.
- **Core claim**: For eligible citizens, especially BTO buyers, HDB is not a downgrade. It lowers the largest fixed cost in most lives, which is the single most powerful FI lever after income.
- **Story anchor**: The DINK + 3-room BTO + JB retirement thought experiment (the one cut from the intro). Plus author's own move from condo to 5-room HDB.
- **Number anchor**: BTO price ranges by town and flat size; mortgage interest vs CPF OA opportunity cost; total cost of HDB ownership over 25 years vs equivalent rental and condo.
- **Singapore specifics**: BTO eligibility; CPF HDB Housing Grant; resale levy; ABSD avoidance.
- **Needs data**: HDB BTO price tables 2025–2026; URA private property index for comparison.

### 9. Why We Left the Condo

- **Job**: Take the condo decision seriously, on the strength of personal experience, not slogans.
- **Core claim**: A condo is a home, an investment story, a facilities subscription, a status signal, a maintenance bill, a leveraged bet on land policy, and a committee membership with hundreds of strangers. Buyers usually price only the first two.
- **Story anchor**: The author bought, lived in, and later sold a condo, moving to a five-room HDB. The decision was originally about commute, not FI. In hindsight, it bought back roughly N years of the FI timeline.
- **Number anchor**: Long-run total return of private property index vs S&P 500 / global equities, after maintenance, property tax, mortgage interest, and ABSD adjustments. Condo MCST maintenance, sinking fund, and lease decay.
- **Singapore specifics**: 99-year vs freehold; en bloc lottery vs en bloc reality; ABSD; TDSR / MSR constraints; rental yield gross vs net.
- **Avoid**: Absolute "never buy a condo" framing. The chapter should leave space for legitimate condo cases (e.g., specific lifestyle reasons), then show what they cost.
- **Source essays**: 001 (private property vs S&P 500).

## Part III: Lowering the Cost Floor

The job of Part III: show that high savings rate is a system, not willpower. Each chapter lowers a specific recurring cost.

### 10. Food Is Where the Arithmetic Becomes Visible

- **Job**: Use food as the most visible savings lever.
- **Core claim**: Restaurants have high gross margins because of rent, labour, GST, service charge, and overheads. Restaurant-as-routine and restaurant-as-occasion are different products. Hawker centre / kopitiam / food court / strata-mall stall is a micro form of geographic arbitrage.
- **Story anchor**: A single dish traced from supermarket ingredients (~S$1.50) through hawker (~S$5) through food court (~S$7) through restaurant after service charge and GST (~S$16.80). Same nutrition, very different cost.
- **Number anchor**: Singapore restaurant industry margin structure; author's own dining-out S$150–300 / month and grocery S$800–1,200 / month figures; SingStat food expenditure share by dwelling type.
- **Practical rule**: Reserve restaurants for occasions; default to home cooking simplified by tools (dishwasher, steam oven, air fryer).
- **Source essays**: 008 (food and eating out).

### 11. Geography Is Part of Your Balance Sheet

- **Job**: Reframe geography as a financial variable, not a lifestyle preference.
- **Core claim**: JB, Penang, Taobao, older heartland malls, wet markets, and where you choose to retire are all forms of arbitrage. The "version of Singapore you buy" is partly a postcode question.
- **Story anchor**: The DINK + 3-room BTO + JB retirement case, fully numbered (if not done in Ch 8); Taobao logistics vs local retail price comparison; a Singapore family that retires to Penang.
- **Number anchor**: SGD/MYR effect on cross-Causeway purchasing power; Penang / Ipoh / Malacca cost of living indices; private healthcare access for retirees in Malaysia.
- **Singapore specifics**: MM2H, Premium Visa, JB Causeway / RTS timeline.
- **Caveats**: Geographic arbitrage is not for everyone — eldercare logistics, school continuity, healthcare access, and SGD-strength-as-bet should all be examined.

### 12. From the Aircon Filter to the Renaissance Household (Skills)

- **Job**: Treat practical capability as a compounding asset class.
- **Core claim**: Skills compound like investments. DIY capability lowers recurring expenses and improves resilience. In the AI era, the cost of becoming mildly competent at many things has collapsed.
- **Story anchor**: TBD — to be drafted from the user's existing older essay on aircon / DIY / Renaissance person (location pending). Should open concrete (cleaning an aircon filter) and rise to abstract (Renaissance household thesis).
- **Number anchor**: Cost of outsourced aircon servicing per year × multiplied across years; cost of haircut, food delivery, basic repairs, taxi convenience added up over a decade.
- **Singapore specifics**: Singapore service-sector premium (expensive labour, GST, convenience fees) is exactly what makes DIY high-ROI here.
- **Source essays**: pending old essay user will provide. Possible thematic touch-ins from 008 (cooking) and 011/013 (DIY bookkeeping).

### 13. How to Reach FI With Children

- **Job**: Demolish the "children make FI impossible" objection without dismissing the real costs.
- **Core claim**: Children change the path to FI but do not foreclose it. In Singapore, the largest costs of children are time, sleep, attention, and parental anxiety — not unavoidable cash.
- **Story anchor**: A composite Singapore middle-class family choosing between "full enrichment subscription stack" and "deliberate enrichment" — 10-year cost and outcome contrast.
- **Number anchor**: MOE cohort participation rates; publicly funded university intake; PSLE-to-university pipeline math; China top-tier university intake comparison; enrichment industry size.
- **Singapore specifics**: PSLE / subject-based banding; local university access rate; MOE bursaries; CPF Education Loan Scheme; SkillsFuture as future-proofing for the child.
- **Avoid**: Moralising parents who pay for tuition. The point is opportunity cost and judgment, not virtue.
- **Source essays**: 012 (PSLE / education anxiety).

## Part IV: Investing the Surplus

The job of Part IV: keep investing boring on purpose, then handle the Singapore-specific complications (jurisdiction, REITs, local index).

### 14. Investing Is the Easy Part

- **Job**: Lower the perceived stakes of investing.
- **Core claim**: Saving matters more than investment return. The default approach should be broad index funds, diversification, DCA, low fees, tax awareness, and boring automation. Avoid individual stocks as the core FI strategy.
- **Story anchor**: A stock-picker vs an index buyer with the same income and savings rate, 20 years out. Or the author's own simple portfolio described honestly.
- **Number anchor**: Long-run S&P 500 / MSCI World return; fund expense ratio drag over 30 years; DCA vs lump sum historical backtest.
- **Source essays**: 002 (dollar-cost averaging).

### 15. The Index You Buy Matters

- **Job**: Disabuse readers of "an index is automatically diversified".
- **Core claim**: STI is not S&P 500. STI is small, concentrated, bank-heavy, dividend-oriented, and local-currency. Useful for SGD income and CPFIS / SRS constraints, not as a high-growth core.
- **Story anchor**: An investor who put 100% into STI in 2008 vs MSCI World — what happened and why; a Singaporean retiree who needs SGD income and uses STI / local dividend stocks intentionally.
- **Number anchor**: STI sector composition and top-10 weight; STI vs S&P 500 vs MSCI World total return; STI dividend yield.
- **Singapore specifics**: SGX listing structure; CPFIS-eligible products; SRS-eligible products.
- **Source essays**: 004 (STI / local stocks).

### 16. REITs: Being a Landlord With a Ticker (NEW)

- **Job**: Show REITs as a legitimate but limited tool — not a substitute for property ownership, not a generic dividend play.
- **Core claim**: Singapore REITs are a real asset class with structural advantages (tax pass-through, mandatory payout, liquid landlord exposure) and structural risks (interest-rate sensitivity, sponsor concentration, dilution).
- **Story anchor**: A retired couple using S-REITs for SGD income; what 2022–2023 rate hikes did to S-REIT prices and dividends.
- **Number anchor**: S-REIT vs direct rental yield after maintenance and vacancy; index of S-REITs vs STI vs property index; gearing ratios; weighted average lease expiry.
- **Singapore specifics**: REIT regulatory framework (gearing cap, distribution requirement); CPFIS-OA / CPFIS-SA / SRS treatment; SGD vs foreign-currency-revenue REITs.
- **Source essays**: 009 (Singapore REIT primer).

### 17. Jurisdiction Is Not a Footnote

- **Job**: Stop residents from losing returns to U.S. withholding and estate exposure.
- **Core claim**: A Singapore resident buying U.S.-domiciled ETFs faces 30% U.S. dividend withholding and potential U.S. estate-tax exposure on assets above the non-resident threshold. Ireland-domiciled accumulating ETFs reduce both leaks.
- **Story anchor**: A Singapore investor who realises mid-career that switching from U.S.-listed VOO to Ireland-domiciled CSPX changes long-run after-tax returns; a Singapore investor's heir trying to administer a U.S. brokerage account.
- **Number anchor**: 30% vs 15% withholding leak compounded over 30 years; U.S. estate-tax brackets for non-resident aliens; brokerage and probate cost comparisons.
- **Singapore specifics**: Local brokers vs U.S. brokers; CPFIS / SRS jurisdiction implications.
- **Source essays**: 005 (US stocks tax / estate).

## Part V: What FI Is For

The job of Part V: refuse asceticism, refuse aimlessness, refuse martyrdom.

### 18. Do Not Become a Monk

- **Job**: Disarm the FI-equals-deprivation reflex.
- **Core claim**: FI exists to fund deliberate spending, not to abolish it. The point is to refuse low-value recurring expenses, not to refuse joy.
- **Story anchor**: Author's family travelling three or four times a year, never to luxury hotels; deliberate spending on meaningful objects, books, food at home.
- **Number anchor**: A "forced consumption account" mechanism — e.g., set aside N% of monthly surplus for deliberate, non-recurring, life-quality spending.
- **Practical rule**: Separate spending you actually love from spending you inherit by default.

### 19. Work After FI

- **Job**: Show that FI is a redesign of work, not an exit from work.
- **Core claim**: After FI, most people keep working, switch work, start a project, care for family, write, move cities, or semi-retire. The freedom is to choose what work and how much.
- **Story anchor**: Cases of people who reached FI and continued (or returned to) work; the author's own current state — building toward FI while writing this book.
- **Number anchor**: How small a "post-FI" job needs to be to maintain SAFEMAX behaviour (e.g., S$2,000–3,000/month of side income dramatically reduces sequence-of-returns risk).
- **Connection**: This is the chapter that ties FI back to the introduction's "buying back the ability to choose" line.
